MoraTea

What MoraTea actually is, in one sentence.

MoraTea is basically a business automation software with a focus on reliability and safety, integrated with AI.

Why you started it: the POI reconciliation / business automation problem you encountered.

I started it because I realised that a lot of people are paying for specific automation tooling from freelancers or automation firm, but more often than not, they already have their own IT employees. The only issue was that existing no-code solutions like n8n and Activepieces, they don't have any obvious direct endpoint to connect with AI. Then, introducing AI also opens up a can of worms on safety and reliability.

What the original workflow looked like before MoraTea.

Initially, I was thinking about one singular platform controlling and routes the user separate VPS instances of their own Activepieces, then I realised I literally do not need a whole platform at all, since I'm only serving a handful of clients.

The specific annoying parts you wanted to automate.

I wanted to create a more direct and straightforward way to allow AI to move and connect nodes together. Instead of asking into a chat interface, you'd just launch Codex, Claude Code, or whatever other coding harness you use in order to manipulate your nodes.

Why you chose Activepieces instead of n8n.

n8n had strict commercial usage, I wasn't about to deal with that. Plus, turns out Activepieces had more things built in, which let me avoid implementing things like commit, version control, those type of things manually.

Why you are building your own control/reliability layer around it.

I built my own control and reliability layer around it because it's really not obvious nor safe if we were to use it directly. You may have a broken version, and when you want to rollback? Nope! Impossible! That's why I'm implementing a versioning system.

The current architecture is like so:

  • React frontend
  • Rust control plane
  • Activepieces
  • SQLite

The technical decision that makes this product stand out is that the versioning is a first class system. Before you can run your workflow, you MUST commit, which makes you backup. For security, reliability, and safety, there will be a separate agent that will clone your workflow and stress-test the hell out of it, in order to make sure things idempotent, failure-tolerant, and whatnot. This loop actually puts the workflow to the test, and makes it much more reliable.

During this time while trying to turn it into an actual product, I found that it's actually incredibly difficult to create an enterprise-grade software. The amount of things you have to keep in mind is mindboggling.

It's still in the MVP-phase, the thing that's working right now is that you can actually define your user's workflow now...
